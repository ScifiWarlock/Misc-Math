\section{Week One Readings}
\subsection{Soccer as Ethnography}
This article is about a professor and his students actually playing soccer with people from various backgrounds to understand how cultures and identities
can be studied through sports and the actions/reactions of the players on the court.

\begin{enumerate}
\item One observation of the players and professor was that fact that cultural identities and differences were put aside during the game
and the Kurdish players and students eventually developed trust with each other
\begin{enumerate}
\item In a way, the professor remarked that passing the ball is a moment of building trust between teammates
\item Another student said that during soccer, one does not need to forget who they are or pretend they are something else
\item Cultural identities mesh together and the sport becomes a form of connection and immediate attention
\end{enumerate}
\item Another observation was the Kurdish players' lack of passing to female students, likely due to cultural upbringings and biases
\begin{enumerate}
\item So cultural identities and differences still can affect how sport is played, but not to the degree of other interactions
\end{enumerate}
\item The article also discussed the different life of the asylum seekers and their desires to return to their homeland and the struggles faced
in obtaining employment and financial stability
\end{enumerate}
